## What I learned (session 3)
- Deriving the closed form before simulating paid for itself immediately. It caught a too-short run length that would otherwise have looked like a real effect.
- The interesting physics is in the *constant*. Kalman refreshes uncertain directions preferentially, but only partially, which lands between "full reset" (2/π) and "smooth shrink" (1). That's a random-rank-one Riccati spectrum problem, and worth a proper derivation if S1′ becomes the paper.

## Session 5 (~02:00–02:20): experiment 002, experiment 003 launched, classical novelty check
- **Experiment 002 (pre-registered):** P1 refuted. For a delta rule, the best key metric exponent is ≈ ½ at long contexts *even without drift*, and ≈ 0.2 at T = d/2. The trend with T is the opposite of what I predicted.
  - Reason: the context-averaged loss integrates an exponential decay, which gives 1/rate, the same budget problem as tracking.
  - This gives a sharper contrast to test: linear attention learns Σ⁻¹ (ZFB), DeltaNet should learn Σ^(−½).
  - The budget model gets the long-T limit and fails quantitatively at T ≤ 2d. Recorded honestly (R-004).
- **Experiment 003 (pre-registered)** in progress: white-box DeltaNet (fla kernel) and linear attention with learnable A, 20 runs.
  - Test bug found and fixed: fla decays before writing, and my reference decayed after.
  - The float64 semantics check is exact; fla float32 is ~3e-3.
- **Subagent 2 (classical novelty, ~168k subagent tokens):** the √ law is classical for noisy small-step LMS (Eweda 1994; Sayed), where the penalty is the square root of ours. Our noiseless law is the squared version, and our σ² = 0.1 data match the classical law.
  - So: don't claim the √ law. Claim the regime crossover, exact gap preservation, and the ML predictions.
  - α* = a holds only at the joint optimum.
  - Updated R-002, R-003, and the 001 README.
