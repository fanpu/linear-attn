# Dense, colourful, many-channel images: what the good ones actually do

*Research note, 2026-09-26. It answers four complaints about the collection: each mark carries one scalar, the palette is mostly grey on cream, the paper is mostly empty, and the pieces read as figures. The constraint stays in force: every visual choice must either render a measured quantity faithfully or be declared as an aesthetic choice.*

The short version: dense, rich images almost never get there by adding decoration. They get there by **accumulation**, meaning many marks that are each cheap and translucent and that sum into tone. They also let **one colour carry several numbers**, with hue, chroma and lightness each bound to something. Most of them add a **second scale**, so the work reads one way at 5 m and another at 30 cm. Every technique below has a version where the extra richness is itself measured data.

---

## A. Principles and techniques

### 1. Millions of low-alpha marks: tone by accumulation
**Sources.** Jared Tarbell's *Substrate* and *Sand Stroke* use a "SandPainter" that scatters translucent grains along each crack ([complexification.net](http://www.complexification.net/gallery/machines/substrate/)). Anders Hoff's *sand spline* draws the same number of transparent dots per iteration whatever the spline's speed, so density builds up where the curve moves slowly ([inconvergent](https://inconvergent.net/generative/sand-spline/)). Tyler Hobbs builds soft texture from 20–100 layers at 2–10% opacity, or from 1,000–20,000 tiny marks ([soft textures](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/creating-soft-textures-generatively)). His watercolour uses 30–100 deformed polygon layers at ~4% opacity, each masked by ~1,000 random circles ([watercolour](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/a-guide-to-simulating-watercolor-paint-with-generative-art)).
**Why it works.** The image becomes a histogram that the eye reads as material, with grain, bloom and soft edges. It is never flat fill.
**ML use.** This is already the honest form of a bifurcation diagram, a trajectory ensemble or an orbit density. The upgrade is that **each grain also carries colour**: the orbit's Lyapunov exponent, the step index, or which seed it came from. Accumulate in *linear light* (float XYZ or linear RGB) and tone-map once at the end, the way astronomers stretch (asinh/log). Never alpha-composite in sRGB, which muddies the result.

### 2. Colour is three numbers, so bind all three
**Sources.** Domain colouring and Wegert's enhanced phase portraits put argument on hue and log-modulus on lightness, and draw contour lines for both ([Wegert](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346969085_Visualising_complex_functions_Enhanced_phase_portraits); [domain colouring](https://complex-analysis.com/content/domain_coloring.html)). Bivariate maps put one variable on L\* and the other on a hue or diverging axis ([Teuling 2011](https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/joc.2153); [designing 2D colormaps](https://trygvrad.github.io/designing-2d-colormaps/)). Value-Suppressing Uncertainty Palettes (VSUPs) shrink the palette as uncertainty rises ([VSUP](https://uwdata.github.io/vsup/)).
**ML use.** In OKLCh ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)), the trainability plane could put **lightness = log convergence time, hue = which basin or outcome, chroma = agreement across seeds**. That makes one pixel carry three measured channels. Cyclic data (Fourier phase in grokking, rotation angle, eigenvector direction) goes on hue at constant L, never HSV, because HSV's lightness is uneven. Add iso-contours as thin dark lines, which gives density and legibility for free.

### 3. Additive mixing of K class hues weighted by probability (the Brainbow move)
**Sources.** Brainbow: Cre-lox makes a stochastic choice among 3+ fluorescent proteins, and multiple transgene copies give ~90–100 distinguishable hues ([Livet et al. 2007](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06293)). The same logic runs through multiplex fluorescence pseudocolouring, where each channel gets a hue and the channels sum ([psudo, bioRxiv 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.11.589087)). Particle Lenia overlays fields as blue U with yellow G ([Particle Lenia](https://google-research.github.io/self-organising-systems/particle-lenia/)).
**Why it is honest and beautiful.** If the colour is a convex combination of K anchor hues *in OKLab*, weighted by p, then confident pixels come out saturated and **contested pixels desaturate towards neutral automatically**. Chroma ends up tracking roughly 1 − entropy. Boundaries get a grey seam with no special-casing.
**ML use.** Outcome basins: p comes from outcome frequencies across seeds or perturbations. First-learned: each digit is a 5-vector of learning times across architectures. Attention: mix the per-head colours by attention weight. Caveats: K ≤ ~6 before the anchors crowd together, and mixing in sRGB (not OKLab) gives mud or blows out to white.

### 4. Spectral rendering: treat a vector as a spectrum
**Sources.** Hyperspectral-to-RGB conversion multiplies radiance by the CIE 1931 colour-matching functions and sums to XYZ, then maps to sRGB with gamut mapping rather than clipping ([Foster tutorial](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/d.h.foster/Tutorial_HSI2RGB/Tutorial_HSI2RGB.html)). Thin-film and Michel-Lévy interference colours are the physical instance: path difference sets a spectrum, and the spectrum sets a colour. Newton's orders give the famous lustrous sequence ([McCrone](https://www.mccrone.com/mm/the-michel-levy-interference-color-chart/)).
**Mechanism.** Take any ordered non-negative vector (a loss curve over steps, a Hessian eigen-density, a per-layer norm profile, a singular-value spectrum), resample it onto 380–730 nm, and integrate against the CMFs. Peaked vectors give saturated colour and flat vectors give white. Where the peak sits gives hue, and total mass gives brightness. **Concentration becomes chroma on its own.**
**Honesty.** It is a fixed linear projection ℝⁿ→ℝ³, the same for every pixel, published and reproducible. Metamers (different vectors, same colour) are the declared cost, as with any 3-D projection. It only makes sense when the vector has a *natural order* (time, frequency, eigen-index, depth). Label it: "each pixel's loss curve seen as if it were light."
**ML use.** `hessian-spectrum` and `weight-spectrum` are literally spectra. Trainability pixels become spectral once each pixel's loss trajectory is logged. For a scalar with "orders" (log-range quantities), a Michel-Lévy colour ramp gives repeating lustrous bands that act as contours. It is richer than a sequential map, and it is a physically grounded analogy that must be declared.

### 5. Chromatic ordering for ordered channels
**Sources.** JWST and Hubble processing assign the shortest-wavelength filter to blue and the longest to red, each filter being one layer. DePasquale: colours must be "clear and separated enough so that you can make scientific inferences" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/08/11/how-scientists-bring-webbs-images-to-life-in-beautiful-color/)). The Hubble SHO palette maps SII→R, Hα→G, OIII→B, putting the strongest signal in green ([AAA](https://aaa.org/2020/06/23/pillars-of-creation-using-the-hubble-palette/)). Confocal depth-colour coding does the same with z, as in Siwanowicz's stentors in Nikon Small World ([Nikon](https://www.nikonsmallworld.com/techniques/confocal)).
**ML use.** When the channels have an order (layer 1…L, epoch bins, frequency bands), give each one its own nonlinear stretch, then assign hues in that order. This is the astronomer's licence. It is honest because the rule is monotone and can be stated in one line.

### 6. Palimpsest: time as overlapping colour
**Sources.** Harold Fisk's 1944 meander maps show the 1944 river as a blank, crossed by the green 1880 course, the salmon 1820 course and the blue 1765 course: "a writhing palimpsest" ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/maps-of-the-lower-mississippi-harold-fisk/)). Robert Hodgin's *Meander* re-simulates this procedurally ([Hodgin](https://roberthodgin.com/project/meander)). Fiji's Temporal-Color Code max-projects a time series with colour = frame ([ImageJ](https://imagej.net/plugins/temporal-color-code)).
**ML use.** Overlay the edge-of-stability trajectory, neural-collapse class means over 250 epochs, decision boundaries across training, or float16 orbits by iteration, with each epoch a translucent stroke in a time palette. A single image then holds the whole history, and it gets denser and more colourful exactly where the dynamics were busy.

### 7. Flow fields and streamlines, driven by measured fields rather than Perlin noise
**Sources.** Hobbs's flow-field essay: step length is 0.1–0.5% of image width, short curves give "fur" and long curves give leading lines, and circle-packed seeds or a minimum separation give even density ([Flow Fields](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields)). Fidenza adds collision checking, 14 probabilistic palettes, and "soft shapes" made of thousands of parallel hairlines ([Fidenza](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/fidenza)). Suminagashi and ebru marbling, and the closed-form "Mathematical Marbling" of Lu and Jaffer, are also sources. Ghassaei's digital marbling moved to the BiMocq2 solver specifically to keep colour interfaces crisp ([Jaffer](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jaffer/Marbling/); [Ghassaei](https://blog.amandaghassaei.com/2022/10/25/digital-marbling/)).
**ML use.** The negative gradient of a 2-D loss slice, or the GD map's displacement field, is a real vector field. Evenly spaced streamlines can be coloured by curvature, speed or the basin reached. **Each streamline then carries direction, length, width and three colour channels**, six or more measured channels per mark. Marbling is the honest metaphor for mode connectivity and for trajectories that stretch and fold (Lyapunov) without mixing.

### 8. Micro/macro: a small composition inside every cell
**Sources.** Chuck Close's grid portraits: each cell is an abstract composition up close, and the cells fuse into a face at distance ([The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/artist/close-chuck/)). The Darb-i Imam spandrel (1453) nests small girih tiles inside large ones, a self-similar two-scale pattern ([Lu & Steinhardt](https://peterlu.org/research/islamic_tilings)). Fine Persian carpets run 500–1,000 knots per square inch, and knot count is exactly the pixel count that makes curvilinear detail possible ([Nazmiyal](https://nazmiyalantiquerugs.com/area-rug-guide/rug-making/rug-knots-types-kpsi-knots-per-square-inch/)). Agnes Martin is the counterpoint: grey bands at distance that turn into hand-ruled pencil lines up close ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/teaching-materials/agnes-martin/grids)).
**ML use.** Render the trainability plane as a grid of **glyphs**. Each cell holds that network's own loss curve (or eigen-spectrum, or weight histogram) as a 12–20 px sparkline in the cell's colour, and the macro-image is still the fractal. This is the Activation Atlas trick at print scale (see §11).

### 9. Texture or pattern as a second channel (the geological-map move)
**Sources.** Since the 1880s USGS convention has given each geologic period a single-ink hue and distinguished units *within* a period by overprint pattern (dots, hatch, dashes) ([FGDC standard](https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/fgdc_gds/geolsymstd/fgdc-geolsym-all.pdf)). Fidenza's stroke styles work the same way (solid, super-blocks, outline, soft). Kasuri/ikat blur comes from misregistration, because the threads are dyed before weaving ([Kasuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasuri)).
**ML use.** Hue is the category (basin or class) and hatch angle or density is the continuous variable (convergence speed). Hachure direction can be the gradient direction, which `loss-landscape` already half-does. Ikat blur is an honest rendering of **seed variance**: offset each ink layer by the measured spread.

### 10. Real stochasticity as grain, never fake paper noise
**Sources.** Brainbow's colour is random per cell, and it is useful precisely because it is *declared* identity-only colour. Stippling (Secord's weighted Voronoi, which descends from Ulichney's blue-noise dithering) lets dot density *be* the tone ([Secord 2002](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2002/secord2002b/)). Seurat's divisionism relies on optical mixing: juxtaposed dots of pure colour go luminous rather than muddy ([Divisionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisionism)).
**ML use.** Don't average across seeds. Give each pixel or grain a *different* seed's outcome. The resulting grain is the measured seed-to-seed variance, which makes it data-bearing texture, and at distance it optically averages to the mean. Stipple and halftone dot density should be a measured density, never a filter.

### 11. Aggregate, then iconify, and let size carry frequency
**Sources.** The Activation Atlas takes 1M activation vectors, reduces them with UMAP, grids them, averages each cell, and feature-visualises each cell. Cell size is optionally density, and colour is class attribution ([Distill](https://distill.pub/2019/activation-atlas/)). Karpathy's t-SNE grid of 50k ImageNet codes works similarly ([cnnembed](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/karpathy/cnnembed/)), as do Anvaka's *Map of GitHub* (690k repos as a map) ([Anvaka](https://anvaka.org/map-of-github/)) and the UCSD Map of Science ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0039464)).
**Read as art.** They succeed as terrain: continuous, dense, colourful, with a macro-shape. They fail when the 2-D layout is read as metric, because UMAP and t-SNE distances and cluster sizes don't mean much ([Understanding UMAP](https://pair-code.github.io/understanding-umap/); [Misread t-SNE](https://distill.pub/2016/misread-tsne/)). The honest variant uses a layout that *is* measured, such as two real hyperparameters, a loss-surface slice, or learning order.

### 12. Palette construction: dominance, accents, and letting data set the areas
**Sources.** Hobbs uses HSB with probability-weighted palettes (e.g. 70% white, 20% blue, 10% red), probabilities that shift across space, clumping and inheritance ([colour](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/working-with-color-in-generative-art); [arrangement](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/color-arrangment-in-generative-art)). Owen Jones, Proposition 16: primaries "on small surfaces and in small quantities, balanced and supported by the secondary and tertiary colours on the larger masses" ([Grammar of Ornament](https://animationresources.org/media-grammar-of-ornament-part-one/)). Manolo Gamboa Naón spends "more time forming a color palette than programming" ([Artnome](https://www.artnome.com/news/2018/8/8/generative-art-finds-its-prodigy)). Golid's *chromotome* is a curated palette library ([GitHub](https://github.com/kgolid/chromotome)). Cherniak's *Ringers* palette derives from Lygia Pape ([Curated](https://www.curated.xyz/editorial/collecting-ringers)). Hilma af Klint's *Ten Largest* are 3.3 × 2.4 m, with a two-principle system (blue/yellow) carried at mural scale ([DailyArt](https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/masterpiece-story-the-ten-largest-by-hilma-af-klint/)).
**ML use.** The inverse of `cdf_img`: *the data histogram sets the area of each colour*. Rare phenomena (the boundary, the unstable orbits) get the saturated primary in small quantities, and common ones get the tertiary masses. That is Owen Jones by measurement, and it is the fix CRITIQUE.md asks for ("composition is nothing but weighting").

### 13. Subtractive and pigment mixing for the cream spine
**Sources.** Kubelka–Munk compositing of translucent glazes ([Curtis et al. 1997](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/curtis97.pdf)). Mixbox handles K–M mixing with RGB in and out through a latent of 3 pigments plus a residual, so blue + yellow gives green rather than grey ([Mixbox](https://scrtwpns.com/mixbox/); [Sochorová & Jamriška 2021](https://dcgi.fel.cvut.cz/en/publications/2021/sochorova-tog-pigments/); licence CC BY-NC). Bridget Riley's *Late Morning* runs paired stripes on white and lets optical colour arise *between* them ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/visit/tate-britain/display/modern-and-contemporary-british-art/bridget-riley)). Riso overprint is a related case.
**ML use.** Keep the ink-on-cream key but go to 3–5 transparent inks. Overlapping measured layers (Fisk-style epochs, per-architecture maps) then *mix* on the paper into real secondaries, so density adds colour instead of black. Additive light suits the dark room and subtractive pigment suits the cream spine, and the choice should be declared per series.

### 14. PCA → perceptual colour, with the basis frozen and declared
**Sources.** DINOv2-style feature maps put the first three PCs on RGB ([DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193)). There are 2³ × 3! = 48 equally valid sign-and-axis assignments, and the usual practice is to pick the prettiest. Lenia shows its first three channels as RGB ([Chan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05433)). Growing NCA shows only the 4 of 16 channels it was trained to make visible ([Distill](https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/)).
**The honest version.** Map PC1 to OKLab L (the eye is most acute to lightness, and PC1 carries the most variance) and PC2/PC3 to a/b, scaled into the gamut. Fit the basis once across the whole series, not per image, and print the loadings on the label. A 3-D projection is fine. A hidden per-image choice is not.

## B. Ranked: the five most likely to fix the four problems here

1. **Per-pixel vectors → one colour via OKLab probability-mixing or spectral rendering (§3, §4, §2).** This fixes low-dimensionality and colour at the same time. Candidates are the trainability plane (log each run's trajectory, not one scalar), `outcome-basins` (p over basins across seeds), `first-learned` (5-architecture learning-time vector per digit), and `hessian-spectrum` / `weight-spectrum` (spectra rendered as light). Uncertain regions desaturate on their own, so the measured boundary becomes a visible neutral seam.
2. **Accumulated, coloured, low-alpha marks in linear light plus an astronomical stretch (§1, §6).** This fixes density and colour without touching the key. *Cascade* already works this way in monochrome, so the upgrade is colour per grain (λ, step, seed). The same approach extends to `edge-of-stability`, `neural-collapse` and the float16 orbits as Fisk-style palimpsests.
3. **Micro/macro glyph fields printed at ≥300 ppi (§8, §11).** This fixes sparseness and "reads as a figure". Every cell holds its own measured miniature and the macro-form is the phenomenon. Acuity numbers: minimum ppi ≈ 3438 / viewing distance in inches. That is ~300 ppi at 30 cm, and fine-art printers go to 720 dpi for 0.4′ acuity ([cambridgeincolour](https://www.cambridgeincolour.com/forums/thread42097.htm)). A 1 m print at 300 ppi is ~12,000 px, so a 1024² run grid with 12 px glyphs fills it with measured detail rather than an upscale.
4. **Streamlines of real vector fields, packed Fidenza-style (§7).** Each mark carries direction, length, width and colour. This fixes the one-scalar problem, and it is the most "painted" of the five while staying fully measured.
5. **Data-weighted dominance palette with texture as the second channel (§12, §9), replacing `cdf_img`.** Area follows the histogram, rare structure gets the accent colour, and hatch or stipple carries a second variable. This is the cheapest change and it fixes the value-structure failure CRITIQUE.md measured.

## C. Things to avoid

- **Anadol-style latent fluid blobs.** Saltz called *Unsupervised* a "glorified lava lamp" and "an average of averages", and the NYT called it "only a screen saver" ([ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/refik-anadol-moma-ai-art-60-minutes-debate-1234774176/); [artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world-archives/refik-anadol-vs-jerry-saltz-2400275)). The failure is that motion and colour are unconstrained by any stated quantity, so the viewer can't find the data. The same trap applies to generic GAN texture soup. What works in that lineage is legible binding: Ridler's tulip stripes are driven by the bitcoin price over a hand-labelled 10,000-photo set ([Mosaic Virus](https://annaridler.com/mosaic-virus)), and Sarin trains only on her own drawings ([Artnome](https://www.artnome.com/news/2018/11/14/helena-sarin-why-bigger-isnt-always-better-with-gans-and-ai-art)).
- **Rainbow, jet and HSV hue ramps for ordered data.** Crameri et al. measure up to 7.5% visual error from non-uniform maps ([Nat. Commun. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19160-7)). Hue is for categories, cycles and chromatic *order*, not magnitude.
- **Per-phase histogram equalisation (`cdf_img`).** It guarantees equal area per rank, so nothing can dominate and the key stays mid. Use data-weighted areas (§12) or a declared gamma.
- **Mixing many channels in sRGB.** Additive sums blow out to white and alpha-over goes to mud. Mix in OKLab (light) or K–M/Mixbox (pigment), and gamut-map instead of clipping (Foster).
- **Unanchored embeddings read as maps.** UMAP and t-SNE cluster sizes and distances are not data. Prefer layouts from real axes, and if you do use an embedding, say so.
- **Undeclared PCA sign or axis cherry-picking**, per-image bases, and "first three channels as RGB" (Lenia, NCA) when those channels are arbitrary.
- **Decoration posing as data**: Perlin flow fields, synthetic paper grain, watercolour bleed that isn't bound to anything. The same techniques are fine once they are driven by a measured field or by seed variance, and labelled.
- **Saturation everywhere.** Owen Jones's rule and the collection's own key-commitment finding both say to commit to a ground and spend chroma sparingly.
- **Upscaled low-res fields.** Density at print scale has to be computed, not interpolated (THE-EDIT's #9 warning).
- **Small multiples of line plots** as the finished object. Fold the multiples *into* a single field (glyph grid, palimpsest) so the sheet becomes one image with a macro-form.
